# Score anchors for Packet Snare

What a 6 and an 8 look like in this game, so scores mean the same thing from round to round. Score against these and the
rubric's general anchors; cite what you saw.

| Category | A 6 looks like | An 8 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Gameplay | Bubbling is fine but samey; risks rarely bite; later mechanics add numbers more than choices | Every few seconds a real choice (where, how long, when to let go with red near); each new threat changes how you play |
| Clarity | Most things clear after a run or two; some outcomes need guessing (why a loop failed, what a node does) | Every element clear on first sight; every outcome says what happened on the field, at the moment |
| Pacing | A wall or a lull of 4+ runs with nothing new; or sectors that fall too fast to notice | Something new or worth buying every 1–3 runs; walls short and the way past them discoverable |
| Design | The tree works but some nodes are dead or confusing; layout faults on some screens | Branches with clear identities, every buy felt, nothing overlapping or clipped on any screen |
| Visuals | Readable early, noisy late; a few overlaps and clipped labels | Readable at every density; threats stand out; effects land without clutter; smooth frames |
| Tutorials | The first card works, later threats explained by text walls | Every mechanic shown before it matters, by a demo; nothing explained twice or mid-run |
| Engagement | You'd play a few more runs, but the late game plays itself | You want "one more run" at every stage, and know what you're working toward next |
