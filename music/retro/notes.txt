RETRO: what went into the track and its sound effects
=======================================================
Source: sfx.js as copied on 2026-11-14 (songs.js, instruments.js, sound-effects.js in this folder) and the
project notes in CLAUDE.md. What all five tracks share is in ../README.txt.

IDENTITY
  Chiptune: an 8-bit console. The colour theme it pairs with is Arcade.
  Key: C major for the lab (songs written with their tonic on C), A minor for the server room: the relative
  minor, so the two rooms share their notes. No `shift`: Retro plays as written.

THE INSTRUMENTS (instruments.js)
  A console's channels: two squares, a triangle, noise.
  Kick      Short and punchy: 220 to 50 Hz in 0.12 s.
  Bass      A triangle bouncing octaves on the 8ths on the chord's root. The songs carry no bass line of
            their own (the stealth songs do: `walk`, played staccato, "tiptoeing").
  Drums     Noise: a snare (the clap) on 2 and 4, short hats.
  Chords    Never struck together: the chord is a square arpeggio on the 8ths, 16ths once the run is
            flying, going up on even bars and down on odd ones. The songs carry no `comp` pattern.
  Lead      A square with an "echo channel": the same line a dotted 8th later at a third of the level, as
            consoles faked delay.
  Fill      A noise-snare roll and a square run up the next chord.
  Mood      The master filter is nearly open from the start (4,000 to 13,000 Hz, a straight line): chip
            sounds are meant to be bright. No drive. Every voice is unfiltered.

THE SONGS (songs.js)
  Pixel Desk (lab)          C G Am F C G F G. I-V-vi-IV.
  Save Point (lab)          F C G Am F C Dm G. IV-I-V-vi, "gentle".
  Boss Rush (server)        Am F G Em Am F Dm E5. An 8th-note riff with repeated notes, ending on a bare
                            fifth.
  Turbo (server)            Am Am C D Am Am F G. The raised sixth (F sharp) over the D chord, a Dorian
                            colour: "a race to the finish".
  Sneaking Mission (snare)  Am Am F G Am Am F Esus4. A triangle bass tiptoeing, a pulse creeping up.
  Hide and Seek (snare)     Am Am Fmaj7 Fmaj7 Dm7 Dm7 Gsus4 Gsus4. "A minor seen from C", sus chords that
                            never land.
  Plain triads on purpose (three notes arpeggiate cleanly); the only sevenths are in Hide and Seek.
  The most tuneful, most rhythmically active melodies of the five. Every melody note on a beat or held was
  checked against its chord: no semitone rubs.
  Stealth songs: the shared hushed kick (50%), clock tick and a barely closed filter (`cut` 0.85).

LEVELS (measured K-weighted; see ../README.txt)
  Overall trim 1.62. Beat 0.701, tones 1.829: drums 2 dB under the tones.
  Song `lvl` 0.87 to 0.91; stealth 1.39 and 1.44 with `rest` 1.29 and 1.51.
  Simple version: gain 1, beat 1.013, tones 1.855, a square voice (the melody too, at half level).

SOUND EFFECTS (sound-effects.js)
  Key for effects: C major pentatonic (C D E G A): the same five notes as A minor pentatonic, so Classic's
  figures land on the same pitches and read as major because of where the music's bass sits.
  Profile (for any effect Retro doesn't write itself): everything a raw square (a triangle under 200 Hz),
  no filters, 25% shorter, and every slide played as four quick steps instead of a glide; noise 40% shorter.
  Its own instruments: `sq` (a 70 ms square), `tri` (a triangle), `chipNoise` (high-passed noise).
  The idea: square pulses, a triangle bass, noise drums, stepped slides.
  Written again for Retro (19 general effects, the most, and all 29 Packet Snare ones)
    Clicks    compute a triangle blip with a chip tick, data a two-step square (a note and its octave),
              research a square that jumps two degrees up the scale.
    Buys      two- and three-note square figures; by panel: plain squares (hardware, legacy), the figure
              and its octave (software, consciousness, Packet Snare), squares over a triangle bass note
              (research, the skill tree). A big buy adds a six-note run and a noise hit.
    Claim     a coin: two notes, three times.
    Error     two squares a semitone apart, one after the other.
    Sprint    chip noise and a stepped slide that climbs higher the bigger the charge.
    Prompts   clean three notes, super six, system eight with noise: the same run, longer each time.
    Summit    a fanfare with a rhythm of its own (two, a rest, two) in a square over a triangle bass.
    Near miss a four-note trill.
    Also      ui, achievement, land, launch, run end, record, breakthrough, phase, ship.
  Played through the profile instead: smash, wind-up, crunch, flow, freeze, release, viral.
  Packet Snare: "an 8-bit stealth game": triangle pockets low, chip ticks, a two-tone alarm, stepped slides.
  Sprint meter: the shared four parts as squares.
  Simple sounds: a raw square, 20% shorter.
  Levels: SFX_TRIM 2.1 (squares are short and thin, so they need the most lift), the simple set 4.17.

CALLS AND CONSTRAINTS ON RECORD
  - Compute's click carries a chip tick two octaves up, like every track's, so it reads over the bass.
  - The arpeggio arrives on the chords' layer here (it is the chords), far earlier than other tracks'
    arpeggios.
