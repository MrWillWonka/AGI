OPPORTUNITIES: making the tracks and effects less like Classic, and better
===========================================================================
Written 2026-11-14 from a read of sfx.js (the copies in this folder). These are suggestions, not decisions:
nothing here has been built or listened to, and anything that changes how the game feels is the owner's call.
"Measured" below means a number read from the code; "likely" means a judgement that needs ears.

Why the tracks sound related: Dark, Soft, Ominous and Retro were each made by re-dressing Classic. They
changed the key, the instruments and the notes, and kept the tempo, the meter, the drum grid, the two-minute
form, the order the layers arrive in, the shape of every sound effect and the mix chain. So timbre and harmony
already differ a lot, and rhythm, form, space and gesture hardly differ at all. The biggest gains are there.


THE THREE MOST IMPACTFUL
========================

1. GIVE EACH TRACK ITS OWN GROOVE (rhythm and meter)
   Today: all five are 124 BPM, straight 16ths, kick on every beat (Ominous on 1 and 3), clap on 2 and 4,
   hats on the same off-beats. Tap along to any of them and it is the same pattern.
   Do: keep the one 124 BPM clock (see the caution) and change what is played on it.
     Dark     leave four on the floor (it is the genre), add an off-beat open hat pattern that shifts and a
              snare ghost before the 4.
     Soft     swing: delay every second 16th by 20 to 30% of a step. Move the kick off the grid of four (a
              kick on 1 and the "and" of 2, a boom-bap or two-step pattern). This alone will stop it reading
              as quiet techno.
     Ominous  triplets against the half-time pulse: let the clock tick three to a beat while the kick stays
              on 1 and 3, and drop the kick entirely for whole sections.
     Retro    a breakbeat or a gallop (an 8th and two 16ths) in the noise channel, and a bass that walks
              instead of only bouncing octaves.
     Classic  stays as it is: it becomes the straight one.
   Where: each style's `step` function already owns its drum pattern, so this is per-track code plus one
   small engine change (a per-style swing amount applied to odd steps; effects that quantise to 16ths should
   use the same offset, or quantise to 8ths in a swung track).
   Caution: do not change the BPM per track without the owner. Held jobs repeat on the music's 8ths, so the
   tempo sets clicks per second (4.13 at 124). A 100 BPM track would cut hand income by about a fifth.

2. GIVE EACH TRACK ITS OWN SPACE (timbre, texture, translation)
   Today: every voice in every track is mono, centred and dry. There is no stereo, no reverb and no delay
   line anywhere; the "echoes" are second notes. That one shared, flat room is a large part of why the
   tracks feel like one family, and why Ominous and Soft cannot sound as deep as their names.
   Do: add a small send bus per track with a character of its own.
     Classic  a real feedback delay at a dotted 8th (the dub chord's echo, tailing off), a little width.
     Dark     nearly dry and narrow, a short metallic room on the snare only. Its weight is in being close.
     Soft     a warm, short room; the Rhodes and the kalimba placed left and right.
     Ominous  a long, dark hall (three to four seconds) on the bell, the lead and every effect; wide.
     Retro    bone dry and mono on purpose. The contrast with the others is the point.
   Where: a generated impulse response for a ConvolverNode (no files needed), a DelayNode, and a
   StereoPannerNode per voice; a `space` entry on each style. The same send can serve the track's effects.
   Caution: check the mix summed to mono (one phone speaker), re-measure every level afterwards, and keep
   kick, bass and sub centred and dry.

3. GIVE EACH TRACK ITS OWN SHAPE (form, texture, dynamics)
   Today: all 30 songs play intro, A, B, A, break, B, A2, out over one eight-bar progression, and all five
   tracks bring their layers in in the same order at the same thresholds (kick and bass, chords, hats,
   melody, clap, 16ths, harmony, arpeggio). The intro and the outro are the same thing: the groove with
   no melody.
   Do: a form and a layer order per track, and a second progression for B.
     Dark     build like a club track: long intro, the acid line opening over 16 bars, a breakdown with a
              riser, a drop. Kick first, melody last.
     Soft     start from the pad and the Rhodes with no kick at all; the kick is the last thing in.
     Ominous  through-composed: drone and bell first, the melody early and alone, drums only at the peak;
              no repeat of A after B.
     Retro    a game loop: a four-bar intro once, then A A B A looping, with a key change up a tone on the
              last A.
     Classic  as it is.
   Also write a B-section progression for each song (a move to the relative major or minor, or to the
   subdominant), so B is a change of place and not only a new tune over the same chords.
   Where: FORM and the layer thresholds are single shared constants today; make both a field on the style
   (and optionally on the song), and let `chords` take a second list for B.
   Caution: "a new game is a kick and a bass" and "the beat plays only while training" are the owner's
   calls. A track that starts from pads instead needs the owner's yes.


FOUND IN REVIEW (2026-11-14; each checked against the code, none by ear)
======================================================================
A. THE BREAKDOWN IS NOT "DRUMS OUT"
   The breakdown sets `drums` false for its first six bars, and only the kick, the clap and the stealth
   tick check it. Hats don't, in any track: Classic keeps 12 hat hits a bar (4 off-beat, 8 on the 16ths),
   Dark its open hats and ride, Ominous its clock, Retro its hats, Soft 9 (8 shakers and the rim). So while
   training, with the layers in, the breakdown is "kick and clap out", which is a much smaller drop.
   Fix: one check per hat line in each style's `step` (about ten lines in sfx.js). Whether a track should
   keep a quiet hat through its breakdown is then a choice per track, not an accident. Ominous keeping
   its clock would be a good choice; Classic keeping 16th hats probably isn't.

B. THE INSTRUMENTS ARE THINNER THAN THEIR NAMES
   Soft's Rhodes is one sine per note; its kalimba a sine and a short octave tone; its shaker and rim one
   noise hat. The same is true elsewhere (Dark's "hoover" is two detuned saws, Ominous's "strings" are
   triangles). This is the part no mixing or mastering can fix: it is synthesis, and it lives in the engine.
   Do, in order of value:
     Rhodes    two-operator FM: a sine carrier with a sine modulator at 1:1 whose depth falls quickly (the
               "bark"), plus a very short high partial for the tine, and a slow tremolo.
     Kalimba   a sine with an inharmonic partial (about 5.4 times the note) that dies in 50 ms and a tiny
               noise click for the thumb: a pluck, not a beep.
     Shaker    band-passed noise with a soft attack and two alternating levels; a rim as a short pitched
               click (a 1.7 kHz sine burst with noise), so the two stop being the same sound.
     Hoover    three or more saws with pitch falling into the note, through a chorus.
     Strings   detuned saws through a slow filter with a slow attack, in place of triangles.
   Where: `synth` takes a waveform, a filter and an envelope today. It needs an FM option and a per-note
   partial list. Re-measure levels after: richer voices are louder at the same gain.

C. EFFECTS FOLLOW THE SCALE, NOT THE CHORD
   Only the sprint meter reads the chord the music is on (`chordAt`). Clicks take their note from the bar's
   position; every stinger plays fixed notes in the track's five-note scale. A five-note scale avoids most
   trouble but not all: a click's G lands on E7's G sharp, a summit's A minor stabs can land on any chord.
   Short, high, quiet notes get away with it; long, loud ones (summit, phase, ship, the big buys) may not.
   Do: use `chordAt` for the long stingers first (voice their chord as the chord the song is on, or wait
   for the next bar's chord), then for clicks: keep the figure's shape and move any note a semitone from a
   chord tone to that chord tone. Keep the tension where it is meant (Dark's flat second, Ominous's
   tritone). The earlier claim in these notes that pentatonic scales "cannot clash" was too strong and has
   been corrected.


EACH GROUP, ELEMENT BY ELEMENT
==============================
Melody, harmony, rhythm and meter, texture, timbre, dynamics, form; then the effects.

CLASSIC (dub techno, A minor) — the reference; improve it without moving it
  Melody    The lab melodies are long and stepwise and work. The server riffs are short cells repeated at
            new pitches; give each a clear answer phrase in bars 3 and 4. Use more 16th pick-ups: only one
            bar in the whole game has any.
  Harmony   Sevenths and ninths, well voiced. Night Survey never reaches a dominant and Uptime, Overclock
            and Packet Storm all end on E7: vary the turnarounds (a bVII, a iv, a bare sus).
  Rhythm    Leave straight. Add a ghost kick or a shuffled hat on the last bar of each phrase.
  Texture   The dub chord is the signature; let its echo actually repeat and decay (item 2).
  Timbre    Three waveforms through one lowpass. Add slow filter movement on the pad (an LFO) so held
            chords are alive.
  Dynamics  Accents: every hat and every bass note is the same level. Add a velocity pattern.
  Form      Give the intro and the outro their own material (item 3).
  Effects   Solid. The eight power-up effects are its best; keep them as the model for the other tracks'.

DARK (warehouse techno, E Phrygian)
  Melody    The hoover lines are the weakest part: they are polite stepwise tunes over a brutal rhythm
            section. Replace them with two- or three-note riffs that hammer the flat second, or drop the
            lead for whole sections and let the acid line be the tune.
  Harmony   Triads moving by step around Am and Bb: right for the style. Try whole sections on one chord
            (a pedal E) so a chord change becomes an event.
  Rhythm    The acid pattern is two fixed bars for every song. Write one per song, and let accents move.
  Texture   Dense from the first layer. Thin it: take the rumble out for the first eight bars of a section
            so its return hits.
  Timbre    Already the furthest from Classic. Add real distortion on the kick only, and a noise bed.
  Dynamics  Needs contrast most: side-chain the pad and acid to the kick (duck them on each beat) for the
            pumping this genre is known for. One gain curve per beat on the tones bus would do it.
  Form      Club form (item 3).
  Effects   The eight unwritten power-up effects are saws an octave down: muddy against this mix. Write
            them: metal and sub drops for smash and release, a pitch-down for freeze.

SOFT (gentle, F major / D minor)
  Melody    Almost all quarter notes on the beat. Add syncopation (notes tied over the bar line) and a few
            grace notes; a kalimba line wants to be a little uneven.
  Harmony   The richest in the game. It can go further: borrowed chords (a iv minor in the major songs), a
            walking bass between chords.
  Rhythm    Swing and a different kick pattern (item 1). The biggest single change available to this track.
  Texture   Pad, Rhodes, arpeggio and kalimba are all sines in the same octave or two: they blur. Move the
            arpeggio up an octave or give the Rhodes a tremolo so each has its own place.
  Timbre    Everything is a sine or a triangle, and the Rhodes and kalimba are sines in name only (see
            "Found in review", B: this track gains the most from better synthesis). Add one voice with some grit (a soft, filtered saw "cello"
            or a breathy noise layer on the pad) so the sines have something to sit against.
  Dynamics  The mallet instruments have one loudness. Vary the strike level note to note.
  Form      Start from pads, kick last (item 3).
  Effects   The most distinct set after Retro. The profile makes the eight unwritten ones too polite for
            what they mark: a breakthrough and a smash should still land. Give them a warm, big version
            (a low drum and a chord bloom) rather than Classic's noise, softened.

OMINOUS (slow dread, C harmonic minor)
  Melody    Half and whole notes throughout: all six songs move at the same slow speed. Give two of them a
            repeating, obsessive figure (a four-note cell that doesn't develop) and leave real silence:
            whole bars with no melody.
  Harmony   Am, Bb and E in every song. Add other kinds of unease: a diminished chord, a chord that holds
            for four bars, a major chord where a minor one is expected.
  Rhythm    Triplets over the half-time pulse, and sections with no kick (item 1).
  Texture   Drone, strings, bell and lead all sustain. Add something short and dry (a knock, a tick that
            speeds up and slows down) so the long sounds have a foreground.
  Timbre    Its bell and detuned lead are good. This track gains the most from a long reverb (item 2).
  Dynamics  The filter tops out at 4 kHz, so a fast run cannot get much more intense than a slow one.
            Give it another way to grow: the detune widening, a second drone a fifth up, the bell tolling
            every bar and then every beat.
  Form      Through-composed (item 3).
  Effects   Long tails are its character and its risk: clicks at four a second each ring for 0.1 to 0.7 s
            with an echo. Check what a held job sounds like and shorten the clicks only.

RETRO (chiptune, C major / A minor)
  Melody    The strongest tunes in the game. Add chip ornaments: fast arpeggiated grace notes, pitch
            vibrato on held notes, a duty-cycle change between A and B.
  Harmony   Four-chord loops in triads: correct. A key change up a tone for the last A is the classic move
            and costs one line.
  Rhythm    The bass only bounces octaves and the kick is four on the floor: it is Classic's drum pattern in
            noise. A breakbeat and a walking bass (item 1).
  Texture   Honour the four-channel limit strictly (two squares, a triangle, noise) and it will sound more
            like itself. Today the echo and the arpeggio together can exceed it.
  Timbre    Only 50% squares. Pulse widths of 12.5% and 25% (a PeriodicWave) would give it three more
            colours for free and separate the lead from the arpeggio.
  Dynamics  Chips had no fades: use hard on and off, and volume steps (full, half, quarter) on the echo.
  Form      A looping game form (item 3).
  Effects   Already written the most completely (19 of 27). Finish the set: smash, wind-up, crunch, flow,
            freeze, release and viral are the only ones left.

ACROSS ALL FIVE: the sound effects
  - Eight of the 27 general effects are not written per track (system prompt, smash, wind-up, crunch, flow,
    freeze, release, viral; Retro has system). They are the power-up moments, the most dramatic sounds in
    the game, and in four tracks they are Classic's notes through a filter.
  - The sprint meter is the same four parts in every track, with only the waveform changed. It is heard on
    every sprint. A set of parts per track (Dark an acid line, Soft a strummed chord, Ominous a tolling
    pulse, Retro a fast arpeggio) would do more than any single effect.
  - Every effect varies the same way (the figure moves 0, +1, -1 or +2 degrees). Add variation in rhythm and
    length too, per track.
  - Gesture: most effects in every track are "a short run of notes going up". Let each track have a
    direction of its own: Dark falls, Soft rises, Ominous stays on one note, Retro leaps.


LEVELS, MASTERING AND TRANSLATION
=================================
What exists: a relative match. Every track and its effects were levelled to Classic with a K-weighted
meter, within about 1 dB, in headless Chromium. That work is thorough and should be kept. What is missing:

  An absolute target      Nothing says how loud the game is, only that the tracks agree. Pick one (for a
                          browser game, around -18 to -16 LUFS integrated for music with effects on top is
                          a common range) and measure the finished mix against it, music and effects
                          together, at the default volumes.
  A limiter               The last stage is a compressor at -18 dB, ratio 6, with default timing. It is not a
                          limiter: a breakthrough swell (music bus up 55%), a smash and a sub drop together
                          can clip. Add a fast limiter after it with a ceiling near -1 dB.
  Music and effects pump  Both go through the same compressor, so a loud effect turns the music down. That
                          may be wanted (it makes room) but it is uncontrolled. Decide: either duck the
                          music on purpose under the big stingers, by a set amount, or give each its own
                          compressor and meet at the limiter.
  The measuring tools     The checks named in the project notes (the loudness meter, the click and meter
                          clearance checks) are not in the repository. Check them in (tools/audio/) so any
                          change here can be re-measured the same way, and add one test that fails when a
                          track drifts more than 1 dB.
  Low end on phones       Many effects rest on a sine at 55 Hz dropping to 27.5 Hz (sprint, breakthrough,
                          smash, summit, landings). A phone or laptop speaker plays nothing below about
                          150 to 200 Hz, so on the owner's phone those sounds lose their body. Add a quiet
                          harmonic an octave or two up (or light saturation) to every sub, as was done for
                          Dark's rumble and compute's click. High-pass the whole mix near 30 Hz to stop
                          inaudible lows eating headroom.
  Small-speaker harshness Only data's click is adjusted for phones. Retro's raw squares and Dark's metal hits
                          sit in the same 2 to 5 kHz band phone speakers exaggerate. Measure the five sets
                          through a phone-shaped filter and trim by track.
  Headphones              Sustained sub and unfiltered squares are tiring on headphones. A gentle high shelf
                          cut on Retro and a level check of the subs at headphone volume.
  Mono                    Fine today (everything is mono). If stereo is added, test the sum to mono every time.
  Bluetooth               Wireless headphones add 100 to 300 ms. Effects already wait for the grid, so they
                          stay musical, but a click's sound trails the tap. Nothing to fix in the mix; a
                          "low latency" option could skip the wait for clicks only.
  Browsers                Levels were measured in Chromium only. Safari's and Firefox's compressors behave
                          differently, and iOS is the owner's phone. Measure once on real iOS.
  Too many voices         Ominous's long tails and held clicks can stack dozens of oscillators. Cap voices
                          per effect name on phones.
  Distribution formats    The game ships as code, so there is no file format to master for. If the music is
                          ever wanted outside the game (a trailer, a store page, a soundtrack), render each
                          song offline to 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV and master those separately: about -14 LUFS and
                          -1 dB true peak is what streaming services normalise to, and a trailer is mixed
                          to the video platform's level. Encode to AAC or Opus from the WAV, never from an
                          MP3.


IF TIME AND SCOPE ALLOW
=======================
 1. A render tool: any song, in any track, at any intensity, to a WAV file. It makes every item in this file
    reviewable by ear outside the game, and A/B comparisons possible.
 2. Check in the loudness tools and the 1 dB drift test (above).
 3. Write the eight missing power-up effects for Dark, Soft and Ominous, and Retro's seven.
 4. Sprint meter parts per track.
 5. Humanise: velocity accents, a few milliseconds of timing looseness on hats and mallets, ghost notes.
    Small, cheap, and it lifts every track.
 6. Song-to-song transitions. A song now simply starts when the last one ends, or on the next downbeat when
    you change rooms. A one-bar turnaround or a filter sweep into the new song.
 7. A motif that links effects to songs: let the summit stinger quote the first notes of the song that is
    playing, so learning a skill sounds like part of the music.
 8. More songs where time is spent. Two lab songs per track loop every four minutes; the lab is where a
    player sits longest. A third lab song per track, or a longer form, before any new track.
 9. Music for the ring's new places. The planned demo has five places (the lab, Packet Snare, The Feed, the
    Training Cluster, Evals) and the music knows three areas. The Feed is described as calm: it wants its
    own songs, not the lab's.
10. A sixth track built from nothing instead of from Classic, as a test of how far the engine can go: a
    different tempo feel, its own form, its own effects from the first note. What it needs that the engine
    cannot do is the real list of engine work.
